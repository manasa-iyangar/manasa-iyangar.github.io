I am a science educator at the school, Bengaluru, teaching Biology and Chemistry across Grades 6–8 and Environmental Science in Grade 9. I design lessons that invite learners to observe, question, test, explain and reflect—not just memorise.

My postgraduate training in Microbiology gives me a strong scientific foundation, while my earlier experience in Learning & Development strengthened my facilitation, communication and learning-design skills. I enjoy bringing these threads together in accessible, engaging classroom experiences.

My work spans curriculum mapping, lesson planning, assessment design, practical science, visual learning resources and student-centred activities. Outside the classroom, sport and a small ginger-ale venture keep me curious about teamwork, discipline and experimentation.

My teaching philosophy: build understanding through meaningful questions, make room for different ways of learning, and help students see science in everyday life.

Chemistry lesson samples

Assessment · Grade 10 Chemistry

Mole concept: worked example

Question: Calculate the amount of substance in 11 g of carbon dioxide. (C = 12, O = 16)

Solution: Molar mass of CO₂ = 12 + (2 × 16) = 44 g mol⁻¹. Amount = mass ÷ molar mass = 11 ÷ 44 = 0.25 mol.

Extension: Calculate the number of molecules in 0.25 mol; find the mass of 0.5 mol CaCO₃; explain why one mole of different substances does not have the same mass.

Worked exampleScaffoldingHOTS
Worksheet · Chemistry

Balancing equations: reasoning practice

Balance each equation with the smallest whole-number coefficients, then identify its reaction pattern.

1. __ Mg + __ O₂ → __ MgO

2. __ Al + __ HCl → __ AlCl₃ + __ H₂

3. __ Fe₂O₃ + __ CO → __ Fe + __ CO₂

4. __ C₃H₈ + __ O₂ → __ CO₂ + __ H₂O

5. __ KClO₃ → __ KCl + __ O₂

Reflection: Why can coefficients change during balancing, but subscripts in chemical formulae cannot?

Other project samples
Lesson plan · Grade 6 Biology · 45 minutes

Animal classification: evidence detectives

Objective: Classify animals using observable characteristics and justify conclusions with evidence.

Lesson sequence: Mystery-footprint hook (5 min); evidence stations with teeth, body covering and habitat clues (12 min); team classification challenge (15 min); discuss misleading features such as egg-laying mammals (8 min); claim-evidence exit ticket (5 min).

Assessment: Correct classification, quality of evidence and explanation of uncertainty.

InquiryCollaborationScientific reasoning
Lesson plan · Grade 8 Biology · 45 minutes

Nutrition across organisms

Objective: Compare nutrition in Amoeba, fungi and humans.

Lesson sequence: Sort nutrition examples (5 min); model nutrition processes (10 min); complete a comparison grid in teams (15 min); address external digestion in fungi (10 min); compare two organisms in an exit ticket (5 min).

Assessment: Accurate vocabulary, completed comparison grid and clear similarities/differences.

HOTS · Grade 9 Environmental Science

Climate, albedo and permafrost

Scenario: Snow cover declines as temperatures rise, and nearby permafrost begins to thaw.

Questions: Explain how reduced snow cover changes energy absorption; construct a cause-and-effect chain linking warming, thawing permafrost and greenhouse gases; identify two data sets that could test the feedback; explain why a single warm year cannot establish a long-term climate trend.

Assessment focus: Causal reasoning, feedback loops, evidence selection and evaluation of claims.

Systems thinkingEvidence
Biology · Ecology resource

Food-chain investigation

Learners arrange organism cards into food chains, use arrows to show energy transfer and connect chains into a food web.

Challenge: Predict effects of a decline in primary consumers; explain the direction of arrows; identify an organism that may occupy more than one trophic level and justify the choice.

Deliverable: An annotated food web and a written prediction supported by ecological reasoning.
